What is Caroling and How It Is Done

Caroling (Pangangaroling) is a lively Filipino holiday tradition where groups of children, friends, or choir members go from house to house in the evening singing Christmas songs. Kids often craft homemade instruments—such as tambourines made from flattened bottle caps (tansan) strung on wire and drums made from empty tin cans—to accompany their singing. Once finished, homeowners reward them with coins, shouting "Patawad!" only if they cannot give at the moment, while grateful carolers sing "Thank you, thank you, ang babait ninyo!"

5 Common Songs for Filipino Caroling:

  • Sa May Bahay ang Aming Bati – The classic opening song sung upon arriving at a neighbor's gate.
  • Ang Pasko ay Sumapit – An upbeat traditional Tagalog carol celebrating the arrival of Christmas.
  • Kumukutikutitap – A cheerful, fast-paced favorite describing twinkling Christmas lights and decorations.
  • Pasko Na Naman – A joyful anthem welcoming the season of love and gift-giving.
  • Thank You, Ang Babait Ninyo – The signature closing chant sung after receiving coins or treats.
